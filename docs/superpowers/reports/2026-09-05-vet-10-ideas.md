## ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

**Verdict for this section.** This is the most carefully instrumented one-lot system I have ever read, and it is trading a thesis it has never measured. The machinery is a fade book on levels whose first-touch hold rate on its own detector is **46.9% [42.0, 52.0] (n=377 resolved, 3 session days)**, with targets the model writes to clear a ratio gate — planned R:R mean **3.31**, median **2.71** (n=60) — and a tape that reaches the planned target **9 times in 60 (15.0% [8.1, 26.1])**. Ninety-five percent of the losers are full stops (**40/42 [84.2, 98.7]**, from the broker's own `reason=sl` lines). Mean realized R is **−0.077 [−0.465, +0.312]** (n=60). Nothing here is proven unprofitable; nothing here is proven at all, and the system's own Stage-A rule already says so. The ideas that matter are therefore not new entries or new exits — they are the measurement that decides whether the book should exist, and the two execution defects that would hurt on a live book before any edge could show.

**Three biggest problems (mine, this section).**

1. **The book can put nine contracts on for a one-contract system, and nothing in the running code was proven to stop it.** [A] On 2026-09-04 from 10:25 to 10:53 CT the broker's own snapshots show a single scenario (NY S2 SHORT) accumulating **nine distinct working sell-stop orders at 29590.50, one contract each** (working_count 1→9, nine signal ids, `nt8_order_snapshots` 10:40–10:53). Price topped at 29543.50 in that window; nothing filled; the 10:53:10 boot sweep cancelled them. This ran under rev `70af663d`; the running rev `36648655` has worked no arm since its 13:25 boot, so it is **unproven either way**. Not in `AUDIT-CHECKLIST.md` under any of oscillat/flap/re-place/gate-changed (grep at `15340faa`); nearest is class 25. Details and evidence in SURPRISES §1. I did not act on it.
2. **The reward side is authored, not measured, and the ratio gate makes it worse, not better.** [T] Planned R:R on executed trades mean 3.31 / median 2.71; realized payoff **1.75** (avg win $124.4 / avg loss $71.2, n=58 W+L); MFE ≥ planned target 9/60; MFE ≥ 2R 13/60 = 21.7% [13.1, 33.6]; MFE ≥ 1R 22/60 = 36.7% [25.6, 49.3]. Winners exit at the target 13/20 = 65.0% [43.3, 81.9]; losers exit at the stop 40/42. The model is writing targets the tape does not visit, because the gate asks it to.
3. **The location premise is unmeasured where it is traded, and anti-signal where it is measured.** [T] `touch_outcomes` exists for **3 session days only** (2026-09-02 22:10 → 2026-09-04 11:49 CT; n=677, 151 ambiguous). RTH-L breaks **82/124 = 66.1% [57.4, 73.9]** pooled, **64/98 = 65.3% [55.5, 74.0]** in NY, and it is fade-armed. `trade_excursions` has 0 rows. The 1m bar table holds 15 days. The system cannot yet rank its own levels and the arm layer is pointed by doctrine.

**Three biggest opportunities.**

1. **Pre-register the level truth table and let it point the arms (the one-month project, §Q3).** The detector, the bars, the touch tables and the expectancy endpoint all exist; what is missing is a written promotion rule, a nightly run, and a kill date. Evidence gate and kill criterion are stated in §Q3.
2. **Stop asking a reasoning model "wait?" every two minutes.** [T] Era: **8,854 executor cycles, 80.8 model-hours, 117 open intents (1.3% [1.1, 1.6]), 3 closes.** Post-0B (from 2026-09-02 07:49 CT): **1,428 cycles, 9.7 model-hours, 24 intents, 2 entries (589, 590 — both stopped), 0 closes, 19 refusals**, average 24.4 s per call. Under `plan_mode=strict` this loop cannot open; it spent 37 minutes on 09-03 (20:35–21:12) proposing the same refused short twelve times. Move the poll to a deterministic state machine and call the model on events; the plan card is where the model earns its keep.
3. **Derive targets and stops from the tape's own excursion distribution, then let the R:R floor be a filter.** [T]/[I] MFE/risk median 0.67, p75 1.87; winners' MAE/risk median 0.37, p75 0.70, max 0.97. A 0.5R-exit counterfactual on the same 58 trades has mean R +0.021 [−0.157, +0.198] against the actual −0.078 [−0.480, +0.324] — an upper bound (1-minute extremes, no slip), not a result, but it says the money is being left at the target, not lost at the entry.

## Q1 — What other bot traders I know do that this one doesn't

I have grounded every "this doesn't" in a line or a query, because this system has 79 audit classes and I found three things I was ready to call absent that are not (regime block, HTF veto, a digest chain into the planner). Each item carries [R]/[I], and what it would take here.

### 1.1 Entry timing

**What they do.** (a) Enter on the *break* of an opening range with a stop-entry, not on a fade of it — [R] Zarattini & Aziz 2023 (ORB on leveraged ETFs, five-minute range), and every desk I sat on ran some form of it on NQ; (b) trade the last thirty minutes with the day's direction — [R] Gao, Han, Li & Zhou 2018, *Market Intraday Momentum*, JFE: the first half-hour return predicts the last; (c) place the resting order **before** the level is reached and let queue position work — [I] a limit that is not on the book when price arrives is not a resting order.

**What this system does.** (a) OR-H/OR-L (first 5 minutes) and IB-H/IB-L are *seated levels* (`kernel/levels_intraday.go:111-139,171-183`), and the armable set is `fvg_entry, reject, breakdown_continue, breakup_continue, reclaim` (`kernel/armed.go:17-26`, dev; the boot line `stop-entry=on(reclaim)` confirms the running rev arms stop-entries for reclaim only). `breakout_retest` is shadowed by owner ruling after a negative replay (F4, 2026-08-28) — so the break of the range is traded, if at all, as a *retest limit*, never as a stop-through. On this tape the retest limit is the arm the marketable guard kills ("level accepted through — marketable, never placed", `trader/armed_executor.go:960`). [T] `breakout_retest` realized: n=9, 1 W / 8 L, −$581.50 (`/api/expectancy` by condition, ids 528, 534, 556, 557, 558, 562, 563, 589, 590) — all decision-path market entries citing a retest scenario, since the condition is never armed. That is the retest-at-market version losing; the stop-through version has not been run here.
(b) The session ends at 14:45 CT with EOD flat (`kernel/session_registry.go:110-118`, `enforceEODFlatAt`) — the last-half-hour window Gao et al. describe is 14:30–15:00 CT and is inside the flatten. Never traded, by construction.
(c) An "arm" is placed when the last 1-minute close is inside `ARM_PLACE_TICKS=100` (25 pts) of the level, evaluated once per cycle (`maybeManageArmedOrders`, `armed_executor.go:188`; cadence 2 min per P3). Part A §3.3 called this "a polling loop, not a resting order"; I agree, and add the number: on 09-04 the arm at 29591.02 was placed and cancelled **five times on the wire between 10:05 and 10:20 CT** (`nt8_order_snapshots` 0→1 transitions), each time judged against a bar close.

**What it would take here.** (a) An owner ruling to un-shadow a *stop-entry* variant of `breakup_continue`/`breakdown_continue` at OR-H/OR-L and IB-H/IB-L (the `PlaceStopEntry` path exists: `trader/ninjatrader/tcp_trader.go:489`), **data first**: a replay on the 1m bars (15 days now) of "stop-through OR-H at +1 tick, stop at OR mid, exit at close" — n per day is 1–2, so 30 session days before any verdict. (b) A session-registry change (`WindowEndCT`) plus a new killzone; **owner ruling**, and I would not touch it before (a) has a verdict — [I] I have seen the close trade well on NQ trend days and bleed on balance days. (c) Code: place the resting order when price is within N×ATR of the level rather than on a 1m close inside a 25-pt band, and cancel on a *bar-high/low* acceptance rule, not a close (part B §5.5 said the same; my number in P6: 6 of 35 arms died to the guard, 6 more to the 15-minute stale window).

### 1.2 Regime filters

**What they do.** Hard-gate direction and participation by regime: no counter-trend entries when the daily trend and the 1h trend agree — [R] Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012, *Time series momentum*, and its intraday cousins; skip or halve on ATR-percentile extremes; skip on event days. [I] Every one of my systems that survived 2012–2015 had a "do not trade today" rule that fired 20–30% of days.

**What this system does.** More than I expected, and less than it looks. `kernel/regime.go` computes `trend_daily` (EMA200), `trend_1h` (EMA50), `atr_regime` LOW/NORMAL/HIGH/EXTREME by daily-ATR percentile, realized vol, VIX (unavailable — no feed), overnight gap. It reaches the planner as **one word in a label line** — `bias: AI x · tree y · regime z — labels only, no MUST on either` (`kernel/planner_prompt.go:257-268, 619`) — and as `AssessRegime → lastRegimeHealth` (`trader/auto_trader_planner.go:2278`), which drives `plans.dark_regime_count`. It is never a gate. `planner_read_facts.bias_regime` reads `up/NORMAL` on **32 of 32** reads (09-03, 09-04). The only trend gate is the **HTF veto** (`kernel/htf_veto.go`, Studio toggle default ON, TF 1h, fail-open WARN) — `/api/config/resolved` lists `htf_veto` as `candidate-unverified` with no consumer found by field grep, so whether the toggle governs anything is itself unverified [B]. The own-tape research is honest and negative: `2026-09-02-bias-calibration.md` — TSMOM-252 "real but friction-destroyed sign edge"; all three signals NOT USABLE on holdout; `2026-09-02-live-bias-replay.md` — every leg NOT USABLE (best Wilson-lo 0.40). ATR-regime × P&L is unmeasurable today: trades do not carry the ATR percentile at entry (`trade_excursions.atr5m_at_entry` exists and has 0 rows).

**What it would take here.** Not a new filter — a **stamp**: write `atr_regime`, `trend_daily`, `trend_1h` onto every closed position (the `trade_excursions` writer, wave 1A, which is absent from the running binary at every trade), then let `/api/expectancy?by=regime` say whether EXTREME days or counter-trend entries lose. **Data first**; the daily-bar history is deep (3,899 daily bars from 2018-12-05) so the regime series can be backfilled for every era trade tonight. [I] My prior is that the ASIA loss (n=16, −$552.43, 2 wins, mean R −0.520 [−1.090, +0.050]) is a regime story — low-vol chop faded at levels that do not exist yet — but I would not ship that prior.

### 1.3 Volatility sizing

**What they do.** Hold dollar risk per trade constant by sizing off the stop distance (contracts = $risk ÷ stop pts ÷ point value); scale participation by ATR percentile. [R] the vol-targeting literature (Moreira & Muir 2017 for the equity version); [I] at one lot of MNQ the lever is imaginary — the minimum size *is* the size.

**What this system does.** Size is fixed at 1 by the Stage-A cap (`kernel/risk_limits.go:358-380`, `StageAContractCap`, env `STAGE_A_CONTRACT_CAP`), boot line `size=1`. That is the correct rule and I would not touch it (the prior review said the same; I agree with the number: lower CI on the mean is negative). The consequence nobody has bounded: **dollar risk per trade varies 25×** — stop distance min 3.75 / p25 24.75 / median 34.25 / p75 45.25 / max 92.75 pts (n=58, excl. 568/569) = **$7.50 to $185.50** on one contract [T]. The stop is `max(anchor+clearance, 1.5×ATR5m)` (boot line, `trader/arm_stop_anchor.go`), so the ATR sets the risk and nothing caps it in dollars (part B §6.3 (1) — I agree).

**What it would take here.** Nothing on size until n≥30 with a positive lower CI (the rule). A **$ cap per trade** that *refuses* an arm whose composed stop exceeds it is a knob plus one gate leg — cheap, and it is the only thing that makes the eventual move to 2–3 contracts survivable. **Owner ruling** on the number; [I] I would start at the p75 of the observed distribution ($90) so it bites only the tail.

### 1.5 Order types

**What they do.** Stop-limit entries on breaks (bounded slippage); MIT for fades; OCO across competing entries so two scenarios cannot both fill; a time stop (exit if not +xR after N bars); partial exits (not at one lot). [I] The stop-limit and the OCO are the two I would not run without.

### 1.7 How they use an LLM

**What they do (the ones that survived).** [I] Three years of this, mine and peers': the model is a *classifier and a writer*, never the *author of a price*. It labels regime and news, drafts the plan narrative from machine-computed levels, writes the post-mortem, and vetoes; entries, stops, targets and sizes are arithmetic. They call it on **events** (open, a level touched, news), not on a timer. They sample the plan more than once and keep only what the samples agree on (self-consistency), and they run a cheap model for anything polled. They put the static instruction first so the provider's prefix cache hits, and they keep instruction under a third of the payload.

**What this system does.** The planner authors the arm price, the stop and the target (`plannerOutputContract`, `kernel/planner_prompt.go:674-700`); the validators then reject or widen (min-SL floor, R:R at arm). The executor is a 2-minute poll of a reasoning model (`ai_model deepseek`, `ai_latency_ms` mean 40.9 s era, max 318 s; 24.4 s post-0B) whose answer is `wait` 82.5% of the time and an open intent 1.3% [T]. Under strict it cannot open at all (`trader/entry_gate.go:160-172`) and the prompt does not tell it so (part C M4; confirmed — the executor prompt's Hard Constraints at `kernel/engine_prompt_futures.go:117-127` describe open_long/open_short with stops "roughly 1.5-3x ATR; sanity range ~15-50 points" and never mention strict). The planner prompt puts its 12.6 KB static contract **last** (`:674`), after the live levels, so no prefix cache can hit it. No self-consistency: one sample per read; fail-closed reads are **31 of 254 plans (12.2% [8.7, 16.8])** and 64 rejected prompts. Authoring latency measured from the scheduled read to the plan row: 6 m 46 s (NY 09-01), 6 m 02 s (ASIA 09-02), 7 m 36 s (ASIA 09-03), 11 m 15 s (LONDON 09-04) — at ATR5m 13–48 pts that is one to three ATR of drift before the first arm can rest. `planner_read_facts` was built to record the model's bias against the tree's and the token count and records **empty strings and 0 on 32 of 32 rows** (SURPRISES §3).

**What it would take here.** (a) **Design ruling, then code**: the planner names the *level and the scenario*; the machine derives entry (level ± tick), stop (measured MAE distribution or the ATR floor) and target (measured MFE distribution) — part A §3.6 row 3, which I endorse with the number in §Q2.1. (b) **Code**: replace the timer poll with an event trigger (touch, bar-close acceptance, news, plan death); keep the 2-minute loop only as a heartbeat that never calls the model. (c) **Prompt**: move the contract to the head; state the mode. (d) **Data first** before self-consistency: log two samples per read for a week and count disagreements on bias, on the primary scenario, and on the arm price; if they disagree on the arm price by more than a stop's width more than a third of the time, the model is not the right author of that number.

## Q2 — What this system does that I have never seen work

I name four. Each with why, and what would change my mind.

**Q2.1 An R:R floor applied to a target the model itself chose.** The gate says "≥ 2.0" (boot line `arm_rr=2.0 … market-entry floor 2.0`), so the model writes a target that clears 2.0 — and because the tape is not consulted, it writes bigger: planned R:R mean 3.31, median 2.71 (n=60). The tape reached the planned target **9 of 60** times; MFE ≥ 2R 13/60; MFE ≥ 1R 22/60. Meanwhile **40 of 42 losers exited at the stop** and 13 of 20 winners at the target, so the book is a coin toss between a full stop and an occasional full target, with realized payoff 1.75 needing 36.4% to break even against 31.0% [20.6, 43.8] (18/58) achieved. [T] Part A §1.2(c) called this "the model solving a constraint"; the executed-trade numbers are worse than the plan-file numbers they had. I have never seen a floor-on-own-target produce anything but a larger number in the target field. **What would change my mind:** MFE ≥ planned target ≥ 35% with n ≥ 100 on the promoted cells of §Q3, with the floor unchanged — i.e., the model's targets start landing where the tape goes without the machine deriving them.

**Q2.2 A reasoning model polled on a timer to decide "nothing".** 8,854 calls in the era, 80.8 model-hours, 117 open intents, 3 closes; 1,428 calls post-0B for 2 entries (both stopped) and 0 closes; a 37-minute run on 09-03 evening of 12 identical refused shorts at 30–144 s each. [T] The cost is not the tokens; it is that the cycle's clock is set by the model's latency (24–41 s of a 120 s loop), that the arm layer's stale-window and placement band are evaluated on that clock, and that nothing the model says on a `wait` cycle is ever used. I have seen this pattern in three shops and it was removed in all three within a quarter, replaced by a state machine that calls the model when a level is touched or a bar closes across one. **What would change my mind:** a month of shadow comparison in which the timer loop's proposals (`decision_records`) and an event-driven loop's proposals are both logged, and the timer loop shows a proposal the event loop missed that would have passed the gates and made money. I do not expect to see one.

**Q2.3 A fade limit that is not on the book, judged on a 1-minute close, that dies when the level is "accepted through" by seven ticks.** Part B §5.5 has the mechanism; my all-time numbers: 35 arms, 9 filled, 6 killed by the guard, 6 by the 15-minute stale window. The deeper reason this cannot work on a *live* book is adverse selection [R] (Glosten–Milgrom for the general case; Osler 2003 for why stop orders cluster just beyond levels and take-profit limits sit at them): a resting fade limit at a level fills preferentially on the touches that go *through*, because the holds are the touches that reverse a tick short of you. NT8 SIM fills at the touch and hides queue position, so the SIM fill statistics — "3 of 3 armed fills printed the authorized price to the tick" (part B §5.2) — carry no information about this. What the system built is the worst of both: a limit that is not resting (so it misses the fast approaches) and a fill model that flatters the slow ones. **What would change my mind:** on a live or fill-simulated-with-queue book, fills ÷ level-reached ≥ 60% *and* the fill-side MAE distribution on holds not worse than on the SIM. Until then the fill stats are unusable for sizing anything.

**Q2.4 A plan whose bias is "a label, not a direction".** `bias: AI x · tree y · regime z — labels only, no MUST on either` (`planner_prompt.go:619`; class 50b ruling). I understand why it was done — the live-bias replay found no usable directional signal — but the consequence is that the plan has no direction the executor is held to, and the arm vocabulary then decides the direction by accident: reclaim became armable on 09-04 precisely because long plays had been un-armable (`kernel/armed.go:19-22`), and on 09-03 a `long/trend` plan produced one trade, a short. [T] A plan without a direction is a watch list; I have never seen a watch list trade. **What would change my mind:** plan-bias vs executed-direction adherence ≥ 80% over 30 session days *and* the long-side arm-enablement on up-label days ≥ 40% (the two-day audit's 4.3% is their number; part D showed it is not reproducible from any committed CSV, and I could not reproduce it either — `plans` carries the doc, and I did not parse `arm.enabled` in this section).

## Q3 — One codebase, one month: the project

**The project: the pre-registered level truth table, and the arms follow it.** Not a new entry, not a new exit. A written rule, a nightly job, a kill date.

**Why this and nothing else.** Every other idea in this report — targets from MFE, stops from MAE, session selection, the break-vs-fade at RTH-L, the regime stamp — depends on knowing, per level kind × session × ordinal × approach, what the tape does on the touch. The system has the detector (D1′, `touch_outcomes`/`touch_episodes`/`level_stats`), the bars (1m 15 days, 5m 11, 15m 28, 1h 107, 1d since 2018), the expectancy read model with a promotion rule, and the seating machinery. What it has never had is the rule written *before* the data, and the date on which the fade book dies if the rule is not met. `2026-09-02-level-kind-replay.md:365-369` already said "the honest lever is time"; the prior review said "let the tape grow". Both are right and both are passive. A month is enough to make the wait produce a verdict.

**What it takes (code, small; owner ruling, one; data, the point).**
1. **Bar retention** to 90 days of 1m (the replay report's lever; today 15 days). Code: a retention knob and the persistence path already shipped 08-28.
2. **The truth table**: a nightly `cmd/` job that writes, per cell (kind × session × ordinal-1 × approach-speed bucket from `touch_episodes.approach_atr`), n resolved, hold, break, ambiguous, Wilson-lo(hold), Wilson-lo(break), and the fade-direction MFE/MAE after the touch (`touch_outcomes.mfe_pts/mae_pts` — note the sign convention: on `hold` rows mean mfe 4.6 / mae −26.1, on `break` rows 26.8 / −6.0, so favourable-for-a-fade is |mae| on holds; label it before anyone reads it [B]). Written to a table, rendered on the plan card. Two days of Go.
3. **The promotion rule, pre-registered on day 1 and committed as a doc** (this is the owner ruling):
   - a cell is **PROMOTED-FADE** when n ≥ 200 resolved over ≥ 20 session days, ambiguous ≤ 25%, Wilson-lo(hold) ≥ 0.55, and median fade-direction excursion ≥ the stop floor (1.5×ATR5m at the touch);
   - a cell is **PROMOTED-BREAK** when the same n and Wilson-lo(break) ≥ 0.60 and median break-direction excursion ≥ the floor;
   - a kind whose ambiguous share exceeds 35% at n ≥ 100 is **UNMEASURABLE** and is removed from seating (today: SWG-L 8/16 rows ambiguous, eVWAP 5/10, SWG-H 14/29 — it flatters the swing kinds most).
   - **Primary hypothesis, stated now so it cannot be fitted later:** H1 — NY RTH-L is PROMOTED-BREAK. Prior: 64/98 = 65.3% [55.5, 74.0] over 3 days. H2 — NY VWAP is PROMOTED-FADE. Prior: 58/91 = 63.7% (n=91 resolved) — the only fade-side cell above 0.55 with n > 50. H3 — DEMAND zones are not promoted either way (56/128 hold = 43.8% [35.5, 52.4]).
4. **The arm gate leg**: arms are placed only at PROMOTED cells, in the promoted direction; everything else is authored, graded, shadowed (the 0C machinery already does "authored + E8-scored, never placed"). One leg in `EntryGate`, both seams (the project's own one-gate law). Code: a day.
5. **The kill criterion**: on **2026-10-16** (30 session days from 2026-09-08, allowing for the 09-07 half day), if **no cell is promoted in either direction**, the fade book is retired: arms off, the system runs shadow-only until a cell promotes, and the next month's project is the stop-through break at the best BREAK cell. If H1 promotes and no fade cell does, the arm layer flips to stop-entries at RTH-L and the fade book is retired anyway. If the ambiguous share of the *promoted* cell rises above 25% for five consecutive days, the promotion is revoked — a rule that only worked while it was being watched is not a rule.

**The evidence gate for the project itself** (so the month cannot be declared a success by narrative): the deliverable is the table with ≥ 20 session days per cell and a signed promotion sheet; the number I would watch every morning is **arms placed at un-promoted cells (must be 0 after the gate leg ships)** and **n per primary cell** (RTH-L NY needs ~100 more resolved touches at the current ~33/day).

**What I would run in parallel, not as the project** (each a day or less, each cheap insurance): the one-working-order-per-scenario guard (SURPRISES §1); the exit-reason column and the excursions writer (§1.6); the ASIA disable (§1.4); the $-risk cap (§1.3). None of them needs the month; all of them need to exist before any live book.
